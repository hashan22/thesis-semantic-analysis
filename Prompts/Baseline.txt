On the evening of November 2, 2020, a gunman opened fire in central Vienna, near the Seitenstettengasse synagogue. The attacker, Kujtim Fejzulai, a 20-year-old Austrian citizen with North Macedonian roots, had previously tried to join ISIS and was released early from prison after a deradicalization program. He was armed with an AK-47-style rifle, a pistol, and wore a fake explosive belt. The attack lasted nine minutes, resulting in the deaths of four civilians and injuries to 23 others. Fejzulai was killed by police.
Investigations revealed failures by Austrian intelligence agencies. The attacker had traveled to Slovakia to purchase ammunition but this warning was not followed up. The deradicalization efforts also failed to detect his ongoing extremist views. In response, the Austrian government introduced tighter surveillance, harsher penalties for terrorism, and reforms in intelligence-sharing.
The event led to increased security measures, national mourning, and debates about counterterrorism policy, extremism, and integration. The attack had a significant psychological and political impact on Austrian society, sparking discussions on media responsibility, social cohesion, and public memory.
