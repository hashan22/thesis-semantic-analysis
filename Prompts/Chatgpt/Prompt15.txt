As a Paramedic at the Scene of the Vienna Attack – A First-Person Account:
The call came through around 8:10 PM. “Active shooter in Vienna’s city center. Multiple casualties. Approach with caution.” My heart sank. We’re trained for emergencies, but you’re never fully ready for something like this.
The city center, normally filled with laughter and life, was in chaos. Sirens echoed through the streets. Police had cordoned off parts of Schwedenplatz, and there was confusion—no one knew if the attacker had been caught or if there were multiple assailants.
When we arrived, I saw people lying on the pavement. Some were still. Others were crying out for help. I remember one young man pressing a jacket to his leg, trying to stop the bleeding. We worked fast—tourniquets, IVs, CPR—moving from victim to victim, doing triage under pressure.
The hardest part wasn’t just the injuries—it was the fear. People hiding behind kiosks, under tables, unsure if they could move. Many kept asking, “Is it over?” We didn’t know ourselves.
Despite the danger, we stayed. That’s our duty. We coordinated with the police, quickly moving the most critical patients to ambulances while trying to calm those who were in shock. A woman kept saying, “I heard him shouting something… I thought I was going to die.”
We saved lives that night. But we also witnessed unbearable loss. And yet, amid the darkness, I saw courage—strangers helping each other, police protecting civilians, medics pushing through fear to do their jobs.
That night will stay with me forever. Not just for the horror, but for the strength Vienna showed in its most vulnerable hour.
