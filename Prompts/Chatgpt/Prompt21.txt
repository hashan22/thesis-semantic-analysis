In the immediate aftermath of the Vienna terrorist attack on the night of November 2, 2020, information was rapidly unfolding. Here's what was publicly known within the first few hours:
________________________________________
1. Attack Timeline and Location
•	The attack began around 8:00 PM local time, just before a nationwide COVID-19 lockdown.
•	It occurred in Vienna’s historic city center, near Schwedenplatz, a popular nightlife area with bars and restaurants.
•	Multiple crime scenes were reported across six locations.
•	Four civilians were killed, and more than 20 people were injured, some critically.
________________________________________
2. Police Response
•	The attacker was shot dead by police within nine minutes of the first shots being fired.
•	Authorities initially feared multiple attackers might be at large and urged residents to stay indoors.
•	A large-scale manhunt and lockdown of the city center followed.
________________________________________
3. Attacker’s Identity (Emerging)
•	Within hours, the attacker was identified as Kujtim Fejzulai, a 20-year-old Austrian citizen of dual Austrian–North Macedonian nationality.
•	He had a criminal record: convicted in 2019 for attempting to travel to Syria to join ISIS, and released early from prison.
•	Reports emerged that he had tried to purchase AK-47 ammunition in Slovakia in July 2020 but was denied due to lack of a license. Slovak authorities had warned their Austrian counterparts.
________________________________________
4. Weapons and Appearance
•	The attacker was heavily armed:
o	AK-47-style assault rifle
o	Pistol
o	Machete
o	Fake explosive belt (used to heighten fear and confusion)
•	Hours before the attack, he had posted a photo of himself with weapons on Instagram, pledging allegiance to ISIS.
________________________________________
5. Early ISIS Connection
•	The Islamic State (ISIS) quickly claimed responsibility through its media arm Amaq, though it was unclear whether Fejzulai had any direct operational links or acted independently as an ISIS sympathizer.
________________________________________
Summary:
In the first hours, it became clear that the attacker was a known radical, previously convicted for jihadist activities, and had been under some form of parole supervision. Early information pointed to serious failures in intelligence-sharing and monitoring, and concerns about deradicalization efforts soon came to dominate the public and political response.
These early facts set the stage for intense national debate over Austria’s counterterrorism policies, deradicalization programs, and the balance between civil liberties and public safety.
