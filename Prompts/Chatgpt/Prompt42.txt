Yes — the rehabilitation and deradicalization programs failed significantly in the case of Kujtim Fejzulai, and these failures were central to the events that led to the Vienna terror attack on November 2, 2020. Multiple investigations, including by Austria's independent commission and counterterrorism researchers, have pointed to institutional, procedural, and communication breakdowns across various levels of the system.

🔍 Key Failures in Rehabilitation and Deradicalization
________________________________________
1. Misjudgment of Fejzulai’s Risk Level
•	Fejzulai was sentenced in April 2019 to 22 months for attempting to join ISIS but was released after only eight months under the leniency provisions for young adults.
•	He was believed to have deceived authorities into thinking he had deradicalized, but the NGO DERAD, responsible for his rehabilitation, never formally classified him as deradicalized.
•	The court and probation authorities either misunderstood or ignored this nuance.
❗ Authorities treated him as lower-risk despite his ongoing extremist sympathies.
________________________________________
2. Inadequate Post-Release Supervision
•	After his release, Fejzulai was under parole until December 2022, but:
o	He traveled to Slovakia and attempted to buy AK-47 ammunition, violating parole terms.
o	He met with known extremists in Vienna.
o	His mentor observed an increase in radical behavior and flagged it.
•	Despite these red flags, no effective action was taken by law enforcement or intelligence agencies.
📉 Risk indicators were visible but failed to trigger institutional response.
________________________________________
3. Poor Interagency Communication
•	There was limited data sharing between the justice system, intelligence (BVT), police, and civil society organizations.
•	The RADAR-iTE tool for risk assessment was not applied in time.
•	A tip from Slovak intelligence about Fejzulai attempting to purchase ammunition was not properly followed up by Austrian domestic intelligence.
🔁 Critical warnings were lost in bureaucratic miscommunication.
________________________________________
4. Late Implementation of Robust Programs
•	Austria’s KOMPASS program, a comprehensive de-radicalization and reintegration effort, was not yet active for Fejzulai.
•	He therefore did not benefit from a structured, multidisciplinary intervention.
•	Even under DERAD’s monitoring, the lack of coordination with security services made their reports less impactful.
________________________________________
5. Lack of Tailored, Individualized Plans
•	Effective deradicalization requires personalized interventions, including religious counseling, psychological support, and community reintegration.
•	Fejzulai’s rehabilitation was minimal, underfunded, and inadequately supervised, offering no meaningful pathway to disengage from extremist ideology.

✅ Conclusion
The Vienna attack exposed serious gaps in Austria’s deradicalization and rehabilitation framework. While some efforts were in place, they were inconsistent, fragmented, and poorly executed. The case demonstrates the urgent need for better inter-agency coordination, realistic risk assessments, and individualized intervention programs — not just in Austria, but across Europe, where similar threats persist.
