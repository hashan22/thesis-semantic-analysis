Testimony of a Police Officer on Duty During the 2020 Vienna Attack
Reconstructed from official accounts and first responder reports
________________________________________
I was on patrol near the inner city that evening—November 2, 2020. It was supposed to be a routine night. The city was preparing to go into lockdown due to COVID-19, and we expected crowds at restaurants and bars making the most of their last few hours of freedom.
At 8:00 p.m., the first call came in: gunfire reported near Schwedenplatz. The dispatcher’s voice was urgent. Within seconds, more calls flooded in—panic, chaos, multiple shots fired. We were immediately rerouted and arrived at the scene within minutes.
As we approached, we heard the shots ourselves—sharp, echoing off the narrow city streets. Civilians were running in all directions, some falling, others dragging friends or strangers to safety. It was disorienting but our training kicked in.
We spotted the suspect on Seitenstettengasse. He was armed with an automatic rifle and wearing what appeared to be an explosive belt. He was moving quickly between targets—seemingly picking people at random. We engaged him from cover, calling for backup and specialized units.
The situation was extremely dangerous. There were civilians everywhere. We had to secure the area and contain the threat. Our priority was twofold: neutralize the attacker and prevent further loss of life. At one point, the attacker turned toward one of our teams and opened fire. He was ultimately shot and killed at 8:09 p.m.—nine minutes after the attack began.
Even after he was down, we couldn’t let our guard down. We didn’t know if there were other shooters. That uncertainty made it even harder. We swept the area, checked buildings, secured civilians. The bomb squad was brought in to investigate the fake explosive belt. It turned out to be a decoy, but at the time, we had to treat it as live.
I remember helping evacuate a wounded man from the sidewalk. He had a gunshot wound in the leg but was conscious. “Am I going to die?” he asked me. I told him, “Not tonight, we’ve got you.”
We worked through the night—cordoning off crime scenes, assisting medics, collecting witness statements. The next morning, Vienna woke up in mourning, and we were still out there, standing guard.
That night tested us. As officers, we train for high-risk scenarios, but nothing prepares you for witnessing that kind of calculated violence against innocent people. I think about the lives lost, and I think about what could’ve happened if we hadn’t arrived when we did.
