As an Austrian Citizen Watching the Vienna Attack Unfold on the News:
I remember sitting in my living room, the news anchor’s voice trembling slightly as she reported shots fired in the heart of Vienna. At first, it felt surreal—Vienna? Our peaceful city? I switched channels, hoping it was a mistake, but every station showed the same: chaos in the city center, people running, police sirens wailing, reports of casualties.
My heart sank.
I thought of my friends who lived near Schwedenplatz, of people just out enjoying one of the last evenings before lockdown. I kept refreshing my phone, checking in with loved ones, watching the death toll rise. I couldn’t sleep that night. The fear was real, not just for our safety, but for what might follow—division, anger, blame.
But amid the horror, I also saw courage: police risking their lives, first responders helping the wounded, strangers shielding each other. I felt grief, anger, confusion—but also determination that we, as a country, must stay united. This act of terror was meant to break us. Instead, it reminded me how much we have to protect, and how vital it is not to let fear or hate define us.
