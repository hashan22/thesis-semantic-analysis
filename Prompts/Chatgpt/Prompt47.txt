The prison experience and prior arrests of Kujtim Fejzulai, the perpetrator of the 2020 Vienna terrorist attack, played a critical and ultimately tragic role in shaping his path toward violence. Rather than deterring him or successfully rehabilitating him, the system inadvertently contributed to his continued radicalization and operational preparation.
________________________________________
📌 1. Initial Arrest and Conviction
•	In April 2019, Fejzulai was arrested and convicted for attempting to travel to Syria to join ISIS.
•	He was sentenced to 22 months in prison under terrorism charges but was released early in December 2019, after serving two-thirds of his sentence.
👉 Impact:
This conviction established his clear ideological alignment with jihadi terrorism, yet his short incarceration did not disrupt his beliefs or behavior — it may have even strengthened his resolve and provided access to further radical influences.
________________________________________
📌 2. Lenient Sentencing and Early Release
•	Fejzulai was treated under Austria’s Juvenile Court Act, as he was 18 at the time of his offense. This classification granted him reduced penalties and protections meant for young adults.
•	Despite concerns from some in law enforcement, he was granted parole without enhanced post-release restrictions.
👉 Impact:
The misjudgment of his risk level—possibly due to insufficient psychological or ideological evaluation—led to premature release. This gave him the opportunity to reconnect with extremist circles, plan, and execute the attack within less than a year of release.
________________________________________
📌 3. Failure of In-Prison Deradicalization Efforts
•	Fejzulai participated in a de-radicalization program run by the NGO DERAD during his incarceration.
•	However, DERAD later clarified that he was never assessed as “de-radicalized”, contradicting earlier government claims that he had deceived the program.
👉 Impact:
This gap between NGO evaluation and state interpretation created false confidence in his rehabilitation. Authorities apparently overestimated his disengagement from extremism, failing to apply intensive monitoring after release.
________________________________________
📌 4. Post-Release Activities Not Flagged
•	While on parole, Fejzulai:
o	Tried to buy AK-47 ammunition in Slovakia (July 2020),
o	Met with known extremists from Germany and Switzerland in Vienna,
o	Showed increasing signs of radical behavior, including online activity and religious hardening.
👉 Impact:
All these were missed opportunities to reassess his threat level. His criminal record and ongoing radical behavior should have triggered revocation of parole or re-incarceration.
________________________________________
📌 5. Institutional Failures
•	The Austrian domestic intelligence agency (BVT) failed to share and act on critical intelligence, including the Slovak tip-off.
•	Risk assessments like RADAR-iTE were delayed or incomplete.
•	The justice system lacked guidelines for handling early release of high-risk extremist offenders.
👉 Impact:
The lack of coordinated response between the prison system, intelligence agencies, judiciary, and rehabilitation services allowed a known radicalized individual to operate freely under minimal supervision.
________________________________________
🧩 Summary: The Role of Prison and Arrests in Shaping His Trajectory
Factor	Contribution to Trajectory
Initial arrest for joining ISIS	Marked ideological commitment
Short sentence and early release	Enabled premature reintegration without deradicalization
Ineffective deradicalization in prison	Failed to disengage extremist worldview
Lax parole supervision	Allowed planning and re-radicalization
Systemic communication failures	Missed red flags from Slovakia and NGOs
________________________________________
📍 Conclusion
Fejzulai's trajectory shows that prison and prior arrests alone are not sufficient deterrents in terrorism cases. When deradicalization programs lack proper evaluation tools, and early release is granted without stringent monitoring, prison can become a pause rather than a turning point. In his case, incarceration was a missed opportunity for true rehabilitation—and became a stepping stone to violent extremism.
