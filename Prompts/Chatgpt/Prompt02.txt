From the perspective of a survivor, describe your experience during the Vienna attack.
Survivor Testimony: “I Thought I Would Die That Night”
As told to a journalist, based on survivor accounts from the 2020 Vienna attack
________________________________________
I still remember the cold sting of the November air that night—November 2, 2020. The city had a strange tension to it. It was just hours before the lockdown. People were out, drinking, laughing, trying to enjoy a final evening of normalcy. I was sitting with two friends outside a café near Schwedenplatz.
And then it started.
At first, I thought it was fireworks. You know, the way the sound echoes off the buildings in the city center. But then I saw people screaming, running—real panic. That’s when I heard it again. Gunshots. Close. Sharp. Repeating.
I turned and saw him. A man in white, carrying a long weapon, moving quickly and shooting randomly. I froze. My brain couldn’t process it. My legs wouldn’t move. But instinct kicked in somehow. I remember my friend pulling me by the arm, yelling, “Run!”
We ducked behind a garbage bin. The shots kept ringing. There was blood on the pavement. A woman lay motionless near the restaurant across the street. I couldn’t look too long. I was too scared to breathe. I remember texting my family: “There’s a shooter. I love you.”
It felt like forever, but I later learned the whole attack lasted just nine minutes. The police came fast. I heard shouting, more gunshots—then silence. A horrible, hollow silence.
After the shooter was taken down, we were led out by police, hands raised, stepping over broken glass, empty chairs, a single shoe lying in the middle of the street. I saw tears on everyone’s face. Some people were still hiding inside bars, others just frozen like statues.
That night changed me. I saw death up close. I felt how fragile life is. And for days, I couldn’t sleep. Every loud noise made me jump. But I’m alive. And I keep thinking about those who aren’t—people who were just out enjoying life, like me.
We say “never forget” all the time. But this? I will truly never forget.
