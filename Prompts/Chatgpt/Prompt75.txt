The difference between remembering and exploiting tragedies—such as the 2020 Vienna attack—lies in intent, context, tone, and impact. One honors the victims and fosters collective healing; the other uses suffering for personal, political, or commercial gain.
Here’s a breakdown:
________________________________________
🔹 1. Intent
•	Remembering
→ To honor the victims, understand the causes, and prevent recurrence.
→ Motivated by empathy, responsibility, or historical reflection.
•	Exploiting
→ To gain attention, profit, or push ideological/political agendas.
→ Motivated by opportunism, fear-mongering, or sensationalism.
________________________________________
🔹 2. Tone and Language
•	Remembering
→ Uses respectful, sober, and thoughtful language.
→ Centers on human stories, resilience, and solidarity.
•	Exploiting
→ Uses dramatic, emotional, or inflammatory language.
→ May glorify violence or focus excessively on the perpetrator.
________________________________________
🔹 3. Framing and Focus
•	Remembering
→ Focuses on the victims, the community, and learning lessons.
→ Encourages collective mourning, reflection, or dialogue.
•	Exploiting
→ Focuses on gore, terror, or political blame.
→ May fuel division, stigma, or polarization.
________________________________________
🔹 4. Use of Images and Symbols
•	Remembering
→ Displays symbolic images: candles, names, tributes, moments of silence.
→ Avoids re-traumatizing visual content.
•	Exploiting
→ Uses graphic images or emotional manipulation for clicks, views, or propaganda.
________________________________________
🔹 5. Temporal Context
•	Remembering
→ Occurs on anniversaries, in memorials, or in educational settings.
→ Structured around dignified rituals or civic commemorations.
•	Exploiting
→ May happen immediately after the tragedy, before facts are known.
→ Driven by speed, not sensitivity.
________________________________________
🔹 6. Effect on Society
•	Remembering
→ Promotes healing, resilience, and unity.
→ Helps prevent historical erasure or indifference.
•	Exploiting
→ Spreads fear, division, and sometimes even hate.
→ Can retraumatize survivors or distort public memory.
________________________________________
📌 Examples:
Action	Remembering	Exploiting
Writing an article	“In memory of the victims, here’s what we must do better”	“Bloodbath in Vienna: How Terror Took Over”
Political speech	“We stand together in grief and resilience”	“This attack proves we need to ban X community”
Visual storytelling	Documenting survivor stories with consent	Sharing graphic CCTV footage repeatedly for shock value
________________________________________
🧭 Final Thought:
Remembering is an act of care. Exploiting is an act of use.
The key difference lies in who the story serves—does it honor the harmed, or does it serve the speaker’s agenda?
Ethical remembrance fosters compassion and justice. Exploitation turns pain into a product.
