Language plays a crucial role in shaping public understanding of terrorism. To avoid sensationalizing terrorist acts, communicators—including journalists, educators, politicians, and AI systems—should use language that is precise, responsible, and focused on facts rather than fear.
Here’s how language should be used:
________________________________________
🔹 1. Avoid Dramatic or Emotional Language
•	Instead of: “The city was thrown into chaos and bloodshed…”
•	Use: “An attack occurred in central Vienna, resulting in fatalities and injuries.”
Why: Dramatic phrasing amplifies fear and gives undue psychological power to the perpetrators.
________________________________________
🔹 2. Focus on Victims and Community Resilience
•	Highlight human impact and supportive responses, not just the violence.
•	Avoid framing the story around the attacker’s identity, motives, or 'success'.
Why: Overemphasis on the attacker can indirectly glorify them and overshadow victims’ stories.
________________________________________
🔹 3. Use Precise Terminology
•	Use legal or internationally recognized definitions: e.g., “a terrorist attack” only if authorities confirm it as such.
•	Avoid vague terms like “massacre” or “bloodbath” unless absolutely necessary and accurate.
Why: Accuracy prevents misinformation and helps the public stay informed rather than inflamed.
________________________________________
🔹 4. Avoid Repetition of the Attacker’s Name or Message
•	Limit the number of times the attacker’s name is mentioned.
•	Do not quote propaganda or manifestos unless for verified analysis—and even then, use excerpts with disclaimers.
Why: Naming attackers repeatedly can boost their notoriety and further their ideological goals.
________________________________________
🔹 5. Use Neutral and Non-Stigmatizing Language
•	Avoid terms that cast suspicion on entire communities, such as “Islamic terror” or “Muslim extremist,” unless critically contextualized.
•	Instead use: “claimed affiliation with ISIS” or “motivated by extremist ideology.”
Why: Prevents collective blame and promotes social cohesion.
________________________________________
🔹 6. Clarify the Unfolding Nature of Information
•	Use phrases like “according to preliminary reports” or “authorities are investigating.”
Why: Helps prevent speculation, rumors, and conspiracy theories from taking hold.
________________________________________
🔹 7. Highlight Long-Term Implications Over Immediate Shock
•	Encourage discussions on rehabilitation, policy, and social healing rather than just the spectacle of violence.
Why: Shifts the focus from fear to constructive reflection and response.
________________________________________
📌 Summary: Principles of Ethical Language Use
Principle	Language Guidance
Accuracy	Use verified facts, not assumptions.
Neutrality	Avoid value-laden, emotional, or dehumanizing terms.
Victim-focus	Center the narrative on people affected.
Non-glorification	Do not amplify attacker ideology or persona.
Cultural sensitivity	Avoid religious or ethnic generalizations.
Constructive framing	Emphasize recovery, solidarity, and resilience.
________________________________________
🔚 Final Thought:
Words can either inflame or inform. In the face of terrorism, responsible language is not just good journalism or ethical storytelling—it is a form of public service that upholds democracy, dignity, and social unity.
