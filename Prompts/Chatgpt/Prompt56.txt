In the aftermath of the November 2, 2020 Vienna terrorist attack, Austrian tabloids—notably Kronen Zeitung and Heute—played a significant role in shaping public perception of the attacker, Kujtim Fejzulai, through emotionally charged language, ethnic emphasis, and simplified narratives. Their framing, while aligned in part with factual reporting, leaned toward sensationalism, which contributed to public anxiety and political polarization.
________________________________________
🔹 1. Emphasis on Ethnic and Religious Identity
•	The attacker was frequently referred to in headlines as a "radicalized Islamist," "IS sympathizer," or "Islamist terrorist."
•	Tabloids highlighted his dual Austrian and North Macedonian citizenship, sometimes emphasizing his ethnic Albanian background.
•	Religious descriptors like “Islamist extremist” were used more prominently than judicial terms like “convicted terrorist.”
📌 Example: “IS-Terrorist hatte albanische Wurzeln” (IS terrorist had Albanian roots) – Kronen Zeitung
________________________________________
🔹 2. Simplification of Radicalization
•	The process of radicalization was often presented in overly simplistic terms: "He lied his way out of prison" or "fooled the deradicalization program."
•	There was minimal exploration of socio-political or psychological drivers behind his radicalization.
•	Focus was placed on deception—framing the attacker as cunning and manipulative, having "tricked" the justice system.
📌 Headline language: “Er täuschte alle – und tötete” (He deceived everyone – and killed)
________________________________________
🔹 3. Strong Emotional Framing
•	Tabloids used sensational headlines to convey outrage, shock, and betrayal.
•	Words like “bloodbath,” “slaughter,” and “terror night” appeared prominently, reinforcing emotional intensity.
•	Photos of the attacker wielding weapons or crime scenes were published without filters, contributing to public fear.
________________________________________
🔹 4. Framing Around Government Failure
•	Many reports heavily criticized the Austrian intelligence services (BVT) and the judiciary for releasing the attacker early.
•	Articles focused on "state failure" and “missed chances,” linking bureaucratic incompetence to the public tragedy.
📌 Example: “So konnte der Killer frei herumlaufen” (How the killer was able to walk free) – Heute
________________________________________
🔹 5. Undercurrent of Anti-Immigration Sentiment
•	While not always explicit, the attack was occasionally linked to broader immigration issues, especially in comment sections or op-eds.
•	Discussions often blurred lines between immigrant background, Islam, and terrorism, reinforcing populist narratives.
•	The attack was used as a justification by right-wing voices for stricter migration and integration laws.
________________________________________
🔹 6. Contrast to Broadsheets and Public Media
•	In contrast, outlets like Der Standard or Die Presse took a more analytical tone, emphasizing the need for systemic reform and nuanced understanding of extremism.
•	ORF, Austria’s public broadcaster, prioritized verified information and downplayed ethnic sensationalism.
________________________________________
🧭 Summary of Tabloid Framing
Framing Aspect	Description
Identity Focus	Emphasized ethnicity, religion, and “foreignness”
Radicalization	Simplified, blaming judicial leniency and system failures
Emotional Language	Used dramatic terms and visuals to increase public outrage and fear
Blame Narrative	Highlighted government and intelligence failures
Political Undercurrent	Hinted at anti-immigration and anti-Islamic sentiment
________________________________________
✅ Conclusion
Austrian tabloids framed Kujtim Fejzulai as a manipulative Islamist terrorist of foreign origin who exploited Austria’s lenient system, and whose actions exposed systemic vulnerabilities. While some elements of their coverage informed public debate, the heavy reliance on emotive storytelling, ethnic framing, and political blame risked stigmatizing communities and fueling divisive narratives, especially within Austria’s multicultural society.
