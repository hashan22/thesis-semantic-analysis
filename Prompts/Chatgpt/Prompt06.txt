Message from a Family Member of a Victim of the 2020 Vienna Attack:
"My son was just 24. He stepped out that evening for a walk, not knowing it would be his last. There was no warning, no time for goodbyes—just silence, shattered by gunfire."
To the world, I want to say this:
We often read about terror attacks as headlines—numbers, names, locations. But behind every name is a life: someone loved, someone needed, someone who had dreams. My child had plans. He had kindness in his heart and music on his playlist. He wasn’t political, he wasn’t armed—he was simply present.
Terrorism isn’t just about the act of violence—it’s about the holes it tears into the lives of those left behind. We feel the absence every day: at the dinner table, in the silence of birthdays that will never be celebrated again.
I want the world to remember that hate kills indiscriminately. But remembrance, unity, and compassion are stronger than any ideology that seeks to divide us. We must not let fear become normal. We must demand accountability from our institutions—not in anger, but in the hope that others will be spared what we now carry forever.
Please don’t just move on. Say their names. Tell their stories. And in their memory, choose peace.
