The motivations of Kujtim Fejzulai, the perpetrator of the November 2, 2020, Vienna terrorist attack, were rooted in jihadist ideology and influenced by radical Islamist narratives, particularly those propagated by ISIS (Islamic State of Iraq and Syria). Based on investigations, public records, and official reports, his motivations can be understood through the following key points:
________________________________________
🧠 1. Adherence to Violent Jihadist Ideology
•	Fejzulai had pledged allegiance to ISIS and was ideologically committed to Salafi-jihadism, an extremist interpretation of Islam that promotes violence to establish an Islamic state.
•	He consumed and shared extremist propaganda online, reportedly including materials from ISIS media outlets like Amaq.
•	Just hours before the attack, he posted a photo on Instagram with weapons and declared allegiance to ISIS leader Abu Ibrahim al-Hashimi al-Qurashi.
________________________________________
🌍 2. Failed Attempt to Join Foreign Jihad
•	In 2018, Fejzulai attempted to travel to Syria via Turkey to join ISIS. He was arrested in Turkey and deported back to Austria.
•	This failed journey solidified his identity as a jihadist and made him more determined to act locally.
•	After his return, he was convicted of membership in a terrorist organization and sentenced to 22 months in prison, but was released early after serving two-thirds of his sentence.
________________________________________
🧩 3. Radicalization in Austria
•	Fejzulai did not radicalize abroad but rather in Austria, especially through online networks and extremist circles in Vienna.
•	He reportedly attended Islamist gatherings and had ties to known extremists, some of whom were later arrested.
•	He was involved with Tewhid Mosque and the Melit Ibrahim Association, which were later shut down due to their links to extremist ideology.
________________________________________
🔥 4. Desire for Martyrdom and Revenge
•	Evidence suggests that Fejzulai intended to die during the attack, aligning with jihadist martyrdom narratives.
•	He wore a fake explosive belt, likely to simulate a suicide bomber and cause panic.
•	His choice of location—a busy nightlife area in central Vienna—suggests a desire to target civilians for maximum symbolic and physical impact.
________________________________________
🛠️ 5. Exploitation of Systemic Weaknesses
•	Some analysts argue that Fejzulai’s actions may also have been opportunistic, exploiting gaps in Austria’s deradicalization and parole systems.
•	His ability to deceive or bypass monitoring structures, including attempts to acquire weapons while on parole, may have reinforced his sense of impunity.
________________________________________
🧾 Summary:
Kujtim Fejzulai was motivated by jihadist ideology, particularly that of ISIS. His actions were influenced by:
•	Long-term exposure to extremist propaganda,
•	Frustration from failed foreign jihad,
•	Local radicalization,
•	A desire for martyrdom,
•	And strategic intent to instill fear in a symbolic European city.
While he acted alone operationally, his motivations were embedded in a global extremist framework, showing how lone-actor terrorists can emerge from local contexts while pursuing global ideological goals.
