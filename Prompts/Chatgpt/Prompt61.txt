Remembering the victims of terrorist attacks like the Vienna attack of November 2, 2020, is not just an act of mourning — it is a moral, cultural, and civic responsibility. It helps societies uphold values of dignity, unity, and justice, while resisting the fear and division that terrorism seeks to sow.
Here’s how societies should remember the victims:
________________________________________
🕯️ 1. By Honoring Their Lives, Not Just Their Deaths
•	Share the stories, names, and aspirations of the victims — who they were, what they loved, what they dreamed of.
•	Avoid reducing them to statistics. Public memorials and media should highlight their humanity.
🔹 Example: Remember the 24-year-old waitress and student who was killed near her workplace, not just as a casualty, but as a young woman with an artistic future.
________________________________________
🕊️ 2. With Permanent, Inclusive Memorials
•	Build memorials and public spaces that invite reflection and peace — not fear.
•	Ensure the design reflects unity across communities (faiths, ethnicities, and nationalities).
🔹 Design idea: A memorial near Schwedenplatz with each victim’s name and a tree planted in their honor, symbolizing life and renewal.
________________________________________
🧠 3. Through Education and Collective Memory
•	Integrate their stories into civic education, teaching young people about the attack not just as an act of violence but as a moment of collective resilience.
•	Schools and museums should promote peace education, countering hate and extremism with empathy and knowledge.
🔹 Long-term impact: Ensure future generations understand the human cost of hatred, and the importance of preventing its root causes.
________________________________________
🤝 4. By Promoting Solidarity, Not Division
•	Public commemorations should actively include diverse communities, including those unfairly stigmatized after such attacks.
•	Create rituals of remembrance that emphasize common humanity over fear or political blame.
🔹 Message: “We stand together, not just in grief, but in hope.”
________________________________________
📅 5. With an Annual Day of Remembrance
•	Establish a national day of reflection — not only to remember Vienna’s victims but all those lost to terrorism.
•	This day can include moments of silence, public readings of names, artistic performances, and interfaith prayers.
________________________________________
⚖️ 6. By Seeking Justice and Reforms
•	Remembering also means learning and changing: honoring victims by improving intelligence, deradicalization programs, and civil protections.
•	Acknowledge institutional failures (like missed warnings in Vienna) so that remembrance leads to action.
________________________________________
🧭 Final Thought
To remember the victims of Vienna 2020 is to say: their lives mattered, and still do. Societies should remember with compassion, clarity, and courage — choosing memory not as a wound, but as a guiding light toward a more just and united future.
