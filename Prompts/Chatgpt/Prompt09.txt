As a Public School Teacher in Vienna:
On the morning after the attack, I entered my classroom knowing it would be unlike any other day. The air was heavy, the students quiet—some looked confused, others frightened. Before starting any lessons, I sat down with them in a circle and said:
"Today, we’re not going to open our textbooks. Today, we’re going to talk."
I acknowledged what happened the night before, in simple, age-appropriate terms depending on the class. I told them that something very serious had happened in our city—something tragic and frightening—but also that they were safe, and that the adults around them were doing everything to protect them.
Some students asked questions. Some cried. Others stayed silent. I reassured them that it’s okay to feel sad, scared, or angry. I reminded them that violence never has the last word in our community—that love, courage, and unity always rise above hate.
We made time for them to draw, write, or speak freely if they wished. We lit a candle together to honor the lives lost. I emphasized kindness, empathy, and the importance of standing together across all backgrounds and beliefs.
Above all, I wanted them to leave that classroom feeling supported, heard, and hopeful. Because while it’s our duty to teach math, language, and science—it’s just as important to teach resilience, compassion, and peace.
