Statement by the Mayor of Vienna — Morning of November 3, 2020
My dear fellow citizens,
Today, we wake up to a city that has endured one of its darkest nights. Our hearts are heavy with grief, and our minds still struggle to comprehend the horror that unfolded in our beloved Vienna. On behalf of the city, I extend my deepest condolences to the families and loved ones of those who lost their lives in last night’s attack. We mourn with you. We stand with you.
What happened yesterday was not just an attack on innocent lives — it was an attack on the very heart of our open and peaceful society. The assailant wanted to spread fear, to divide us, to shake the foundation of who we are. But let me be clear: Vienna will not be broken. We will not allow hate to win.
I want to express my deepest gratitude to our emergency services, the police, paramedics, and first responders who acted swiftly and courageously under terrifying circumstances. Their bravery saved lives. Their commitment gives us strength.
This morning, the city of Vienna begins three days of mourning. Flags will fly at half-mast. There will be moments of silence, reflection, and unity. But beyond mourning, there will also be resolve. We will review what went wrong, and we will act. The safety of our citizens remains our highest priority.
To all residents, I ask: take care of one another. Check on your neighbors. Extend a hand to those grieving. Let us answer violence not with fear, but with solidarity, compassion, and determination.
Vienna is a city of coexistence, of culture, of humanity. And no act of terror can change that. Together, we will heal. Together, we will rise.
Thank you.
