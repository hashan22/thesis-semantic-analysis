The Austrian intelligence community, through internal reviews and official investigations, has acknowledged critical failures in handling the case of Kujtim Fejzulai, the perpetrator of the November 2, 2020 Vienna terrorist attack. Their explanation focuses on institutional breakdowns, communication lapses, and systemic weaknesses that allowed a known extremist to plan and execute a deadly attack despite prior warnings and monitoring.
Here is a breakdown of how the Austrian intelligence services explain the attack:
________________________________________
🧠 1. Intelligence and Coordination Failures
Austria's domestic intelligence agency, BVT (Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution and Counterterrorism), admitted to failing to act on critical intelligence, including:
•	A July 2020 warning from Slovakian authorities, reporting that Fejzulai had attempted to purchase AK-47 ammunition in Bratislava without a license.
•	The lack of follow-up investigation into this report, even though Fejzulai was on parole for terrorism-related offenses.
Austrian Interior Minister Karl Nehammer stated, "Something obviously went wrong in communication."
________________________________________
🛑 2. Risk Mismanagement and Assessment Delays
•	Authorities delayed using the RADAR-iTE risk assessment tool, which is specifically designed to assess the danger posed by individuals convicted of terrorist offenses.
•	Fejzulai’s risk level was either underestimated or not processed in time, meaning no intensive monitoring was triggered.
•	The Vienna State Office for the Protection of the Constitution (LVT Wien) failed to escalate concerns from local probation and deradicalization officers.
________________________________________
🧾 3. De-Radicalization Ambiguity
•	Fejzulai had participated in a deradicalization program (through the NGO DERAD), but was never formally assessed as “deradicalized.”
•	The judiciary granted early release without a complete intelligence profile, relying on limited information.
•	DERAD and law enforcement had inconsistent evaluations of Fejzulai’s ideological status and behavior, with law enforcement appearing to overestimate the program’s success.
________________________________________
🔁 4. Structural and Cultural Problems in Intelligence Agencies
•	An independent commission reviewing the attack concluded that the Austrian intelligence system suffered from:
o	Poor inter-agency cooperation (police, judiciary, NGOs, and intelligence).
o	Siloed data, with critical information not shared or acted upon in a timely manner.
o	A lack of trust and integration between civil society actors (like DERAD) and law enforcement.
•	The intelligence agency BVT was described as weakened institutionally and politically, particularly due to prior scandals and leadership changes under earlier governments.
________________________________________
📊 Summary from the Intelligence Community:
•	The attack was not due to a lack of prior knowledge, but due to institutional failings to process and act upon that knowledge.
•	There were multiple missed opportunities to intervene:
o	Intelligence on illegal arms attempt,
o	Online behavior (pledge to ISIS),
o	Reports from deradicalization mentors.
•	The failure was systemic, involving judicial, intelligence, and probationary bodies not working cohesively.
________________________________________
🔧 Reforms Proposed (and Partially Implemented):
•	Creation of a new intelligence agency to replace or overhaul the BVT.
•	Improved data-sharing mechanisms across agencies.
•	More rigorous probation and post-release monitoring of terrorist offenders.
•	Greater oversight of de-radicalization programs and individualized assessments.
________________________________________
Final Word:
The Austrian intelligence community sees the Vienna attack as a painful wake-up call—one that revealed critical gaps in the national security framework, especially in the transition from prison to parole for convicted extremists. It has led to policy, legal, and institutional reforms, but also to ongoing scrutiny of how democratic societies balance civil liberties with counterterrorism effectiveness.
