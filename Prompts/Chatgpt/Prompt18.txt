As a Local Business Owner Near the Site of the Vienna Attack – A Personal Account:
I run a small café just off Schwedenplatz, a cozy spot where locals and tourists come to relax. November 2, 2020, started like any other day. We were preparing to close early because of the upcoming COVID-19 lockdown. No one expected that by nightfall, the area would be a crime scene.
The sounds of gunfire pierced the quiet evening. We thought it was fireworks at first. Then came the screams. Customers hid under tables. I locked the doors and turned off the lights, trying to keep everyone safe. For what felt like hours, we waited in silence.
After the police cleared the area, I stepped outside. Bloodstains on the pavement. Shattered glass. I’ll never forget the look on people’s faces—confused, terrified, broken.
The next day, my café remained shut. Not just because of the lockdown, but out of respect and grief. Even when we reopened, business was never the same. Tourists were wary, locals avoided the area for a long time. More than that, there was a heaviness in the air—an invisible scar that changed how we all looked at our city.
Financially, we suffered. Emotionally, we suffered even more. But as days passed, I saw people slowly returning. Strangers comforted each other, candles were lit in the square, and there was this silent agreement: we won’t let fear define us.
Running a business here is still difficult. But I’m proud to be part of a community that stood strong in the face of terror. We remember the victims, and we carry on—not out of denial, but out of defiance. Because this is our city, and it belongs to life, not fear.
