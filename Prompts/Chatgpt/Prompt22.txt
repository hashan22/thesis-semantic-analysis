In the week following the November 2, 2020 terrorist attack in Vienna, public opinion in Austria underwent a notable and multifaceted shift—marked by grief, anger, political polarization, and a growing debate on security and civil liberties.
________________________________________
1. Initial Reaction: Shock and Solidarity
•	In the first few days, national mourning united the country.
•	Vigils, moments of silence, and widespread expressions of solidarity with victims and their families dominated social and traditional media.
•	Hashtags like #ViennaStrong and #PrayForVienna trended globally.
•	There was an overwhelming show of unity across ethnic and religious lines, with Muslim organizations openly condemning the attack.
________________________________________
2. Rising Fear and Distrust
•	As more details emerged—that the attacker was a known jihadist previously convicted and released early—public trust in the intelligence and justice systems was shaken.
•	Many Austrians began questioning how a convicted extremist could:
o	Join a deradicalization program,
o	Travel to another country to attempt to buy ammunition,
o	Post radical content online,
— and still not raise sufficient alarm.
________________________________________
3. Political Polarization
•	The attack amplified existing political divides:
o	Right-wing and populist voices called for stricter immigration controls, mosque closures, and a clampdown on "political Islam."
o	Centrist and liberal groups warned against collective blame and the erosion of civil rights.
•	Public debates intensified about freedom of religion vs. national security, especially around proposed government measures like:
o	Electronic monitoring of ex-convicts,
o	A register for imams,
o	Indefinite detention for extremist offenders.
________________________________________
4. Growing Scrutiny of Deradicalization Efforts
•	There was widespread skepticism about the effectiveness of Austria’s deradicalization programs.
•	NGOs like DERAD were forced to publicly clarify that the attacker had never been assessed as de-radicalized, contradicting initial government claims.
•	Many citizens began demanding reform or replacement of existing systems that failed to detect the continuing threat.
________________________________________
5. Islamophobia and Community Reactions
•	While many Austrians continued to support interfaith solidarity, reports of Islamophobic incidents increased—ranging from verbal abuse to vandalism.
•	Members of the Muslim community expressed fear of being collectively blamed and scapegoated, especially amid heightened media scrutiny.
________________________________________
Summary:
By the end of the week, public opinion had shifted from initial unity and grief to a more fractured and complex landscape: calls for accountability and reform were growing louder, while fears of rising extremism were counterbalanced by concerns about reactionary policies, religious discrimination, and erosion of democratic values.
This pivotal week shaped Austria’s ongoing debate on balancing security with freedom in the face of terrorism.
