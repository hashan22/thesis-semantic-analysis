As a First Responder During the 2020 Vienna Attack: A Personal Account
The night of November 2, 2020, is etched into my memory—chaotic, deafening, and heavy with the smell of smoke and fear.
I was on duty near Schwedenplatz when the first emergency call came in: shots fired, multiple injuries. My training kicked in immediately. We rushed to the scene, unsure how many attackers there were. At that moment, we only knew one thing: lives were at stake.
People were running, some crying, others frozen in shock. The streets that were so alive just minutes before were now scenes of devastation. I remember shielding a young woman who had been hit, applying pressure to her wound while trying to keep her conscious. Around us, gunfire echoed. My partner coordinated with police units while I stayed with the injured until paramedics arrived.
We had to make fast decisions—who to move first, which areas were cleared, whether it was safe for medics to approach. Despite the chaos, we worked as a unit. The courage shown by everyone—police, medics, even bystanders who tried to help—is something I’ll never forget.
After the shooting stopped, the streets were eerily quiet. But the work didn’t end. We continued assisting victims, identifying the deceased, and helping with scene control and witness statements.
Handling the emergency was about more than training. It was about staying calm, being present, and showing humanity when the world around us had just been shattered. I carry the faces of those we couldn’t save—and the ones we did—with me every single day.
