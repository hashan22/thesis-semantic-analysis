The Islamic State (ISIS) was involved and referenced in the Vienna attack on November 2, 2020, primarily through the attacker's inspiration, ideology, and the group's subsequent claim of responsibility. Here's an overview of ISIS's role and connection to the attack:

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Attacker's Inspiration| Kujtim Fejzulai, the perpetrator, was inspired by ISIS ideology and propaganda. He had attempted to travel to Syria to join the group in 2019 and remained influenced by its extremist narrative. |
| Ideological Influence| Fejzulai's radical interpretation of Islam and justification for violence were shaped by ISIS propaganda, which promotes a militant and extremist worldview. |
| Online Radicalization| Fejzulai's radicalization was significantly influenced by online ISIS content, including propaganda videos, social media interactions, and encrypted communication with like-minded individuals. |
| Claim of Responsibility| Shortly after the attack, ISIS claimed responsibility through its Amaq News Agency, stating that the perpetrator was a "soldier of the caliphate" who carried out the attack in response to the group's calls for violence. |
| Tactical Influence| The attack's modus operandi, including the use of firearms, a fake explosive belt, and targeting of public spaces, bore similarities to previous ISIS-inspired and directed attacks in Europe. |
| Symbolic Connection| The timing of the attack, just before a nationwide COVID-19 lockdown, and the targeting of Vienna's city center, may have been intended to maximize its symbolic impact and media coverage, aligning with ISIS's strategy of inspiring and directing high-profile attacks. |

Key Findings and Context
1. Attacker's Inspiration:
	 Fejzulai's inspiration by ISIS ideology and propaganda played a significant role in his radicalization and decision to carry out the attack. His attempt to join the group in Syria demonstrated his commitment to its extremist cause.
2. Ideological Influence:
	 ISIS's ideological influence on Fejzulai was evident in his radical interpretation of Islam and justification for violence. The group's propaganda and narrative provided him with the ideological foundation and motivation for his actions.
3. Online Radicalization:
	 Fejzulai's online radicalization was significantly influenced by ISIS content, which he consumed and shared through various platforms. This process underscores the importance of addressing online radicalization and extremist content.
4. Claim of Responsibility:
	 ISIS's claim of responsibility for the attack, through its Amaq News Agency, suggested a connection between the group and the perpetrator. However, the exact nature and extent of this connection remain subject to investigation and debate.
5. Tactical Influence:
	 The attack's tactical similarities to previous ISIS-inspired and directed attacks in Europe highlighted the group's influence on the perpetrator's planning and execution of the incident.
6. Symbolic Connection:
	 The timing and targeting of the attack may have been intended to maximize its symbolic impact and media coverage, aligning with ISIS's strategy of inspiring and directing high-profile incidents.

Conclusion
ISIS was involved and referenced in the Vienna attack through the attacker's inspiration, ideological influence, online radicalization, claim of responsibility, tactical influence, and symbolic connection. Understanding the group's role and connection to the incident is essential for developing effective counterterrorism strategies that address the root causes of extremism, promote social cohesion and resilience, and foster a more peaceful, secure, and inclusive society. By learning from the Vienna attack and its aftermath, Austria and the international community can work together to prevent similar incidents and build a brighter future for all.
