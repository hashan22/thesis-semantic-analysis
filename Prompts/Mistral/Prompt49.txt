The framing of "terrorism" has evolved significantly through events like the Vienna attack, reflecting changes in the socio-political landscape, the nature of the terrorist threat, and the public discourse surrounding these issues. Here are some key ways in which the framing of terrorism has evolved:

| Evolution | Details | Implications |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| From Foreign to Domestic Threat| The framing of terrorism has shifted from a focus on foreign threats and international networks to an emphasis on domestic threats and lone wolf attackers. This evolution reflects the changing nature of the terrorist threat, with an increasing number of attacks carried out by individuals radicalized within their own countries. | Highlights the need for a more comprehensive and proactive approach to counterterrorism that addresses the root causes of radicalization, promotes social cohesion and resilience, and fosters a more inclusive and cohesive society. |
| From Ideological to Personal Motivations| The framing of terrorism has evolved to recognize the complex and multifaceted nature of the radicalization process, encompassing not only ideological factors but also personal motivations, such as feelings of alienation, marginalization, and grievance. | Emphasizes the importance of addressing the social, economic, and political factors that contribute to radicalization, as well as promoting a more nuanced and informed understanding of the complex interplay between ideology and personal experiences. |
| From Religious to Political and Social Grievances| The framing of terrorism has expanded to include not only religious extremism but also political and social grievances, such as perceptions of injustice, inequality, and oppression. This evolution reflects the diverse and multifaceted nature of the terrorist threat, as well as the need for a more holistic and integrated approach to addressing the root causes of violence and extremism. | Highlights the need for a more comprehensive and proactive approach to counterterrorism that addresses the political, social, and economic factors that contribute to radicalization, as well as promoting a more just, inclusive, and peaceful society. |
| From Military to Law Enforcement Response| The framing of terrorism has evolved to emphasize the role of law enforcement and intelligence agencies in detecting, preventing, and responding to terrorist threats, rather than relying solely on military interventions. This shift reflects the changing nature of the terrorist threat, with an increasing focus on domestic threats and lone wolf attackers. | Underscores the importance of investing in advanced technologies and techniques for detecting and disrupting terrorist plots, as well as promoting cooperation and coordination among law enforcement and intelligence agencies at the national and international levels. |
| From Reactive to Proactive Approach| The framing of terrorism has shifted from a reactive approach focused on responding to attacks after they occur to a proactive approach aimed at preventing radicalization and detecting potential threats before they materialize. | From Security to Human Rights Focus| The framing of terrorism has evolved to recognize the importance of balancing security concerns with the protection of human rights and civil liberties. | From National to International Cooperation| The framing of terrorism has expanded to recognize the global nature of the terrorist threat and the need for international cooperation and coordination in addressing this challenge. Key Findings and Reflections

1. From Foreign to Domestic Threat:
	 The shift in the framing of terrorism from a focus on foreign threats to an emphasis on domestic threats reflects the changing nature of the terrorist threat, with an increasing number of attacks carried out by individuals radicalized within their own countries.
	 This evolution highlights the need for a more comprehensive and proactive approach to counterterrorism that addresses the root causes of radicalization, promotes social cohesion and resilience, and fosters a more inclusive and cohesive society.
2. From Ideological to Personal Motivations:
	 The recognition of the complex and multifaceted nature of the radicalization process, encompassing both ideological factors and personal motivations, emphasizes the importance of addressing the social, economic, and political factors that contribute to radicalization.
	 This evolution underscores the need for a more nuanced and informed understanding of the complex interplay between ideology and personal experiences, as well as the importance of promoting a more holistic and integrated approach to addressing the root causes of violence and extremism.
3. From Religious to Political and Social Grievances:
	 The expansion of the framing of terrorism to include political and social grievances reflects the diverse and multifaceted nature of the terrorist threat, as well as the need for a more comprehensive and proactive approach to addressing the root causes of radicalization.
	 This evolution highlights the importance of promoting a more just, inclusive, and peaceful society, as well as addressing the political, social, and economic factors that contribute to feelings of injustice, inequality, and oppression.
4. From Military to Law Enforcement Response:
	 The shift in the framing of terrorism from a focus on military interventions to an emphasis on law enforcement and intelligence agencies reflects the changing nature of the terrorist threat, with an increasing focus on domestic threats and lone wolf attackers.
	 This evolution underscores the importance of investing in advanced technologies and techniques for detecting and disrupting terrorist plots, as well as promoting cooperation and coordination among law enforcement and intelligence agencies at the national and international levels.
5. From Reactive to Proactive Approach:
	 The shift in the framing of terrorism from a reactive approach to a proactive approach emphasizes the importance of preventing radicalization and detecting potential threats before they materialize.
	 This evolution highlights the need for investing in community engagement and resilience programs, educational initiatives, and support for grassroots organizations that promote dialogue, understanding, and cooperation among diverse communities.
6. From Security to Human Rights Focus:
	 The recognition of the importance of balancing security concerns with the protection of human rights and civil liberties reflects the growing awareness of the potential for counterterrorism measures to infringe upon individual rights and freedoms.
	 This evolution emphasizes the importance of promoting transparent, accountable, and responsive governance that addresses the concerns and aspirations of all citizens, as well as fostering a sense of trust, cooperation, and shared responsibility among diverse communities.
7. From National to International Cooperation:
	 The expansion of the framing of terrorism to recognize the global nature of the terrorist threat and the need for international cooperation and coordination reflects the increasing interconnectedness and interdependence of the world.
	 This evolution highlights the importance of enhancing partnerships and collaboration with international counterparts, as well as supporting global efforts to combat extremism and terrorism and promote a more peaceful and secure world.

Conclusion

The framing of "terrorism" has evolved significantly through events like the Vienna attack, reflecting changes in the socio-political landscape, the nature of the terrorist threat, and the public discourse surrounding these issues. These evolutions highlight the need for a more comprehensive and proactive approach to counterterrorism that addresses the root causes of radicalization, promotes social cohesion and resilience, and fosters a more inclusive, just, and peaceful society. By learning from these evolutions and implementing the necessary reforms and improvements, Austria and the international community can work together to prevent similar incidents and build a brighter future for all. This includes investing in advanced technologies and techniques for detecting and disrupting terrorist plots, promoting cooperation and coordination among law enforcement and intelligence agencies, addressing the political, social, and economic factors that contribute to radicalization, and enhancing partnerships and collaboration with international counterparts.
