|---------------------|---------|--------------|

| Inadequate Monitoring of Known Extremists| The attacker, Kujtim Fejzulai, was a known extremist with a history of radicalization and attempts to join ISIS. Key Findings and Recommendations

1. Improved Intelligence Sharing:
	 The lack of effective intelligence sharing among security agencies was a significant factor in the Vienna attack, highlighting the need for improved coordination and communication.
	 The establishment of a centralized intelligence-sharing platform and regular joint exercises and training programs can help to enhance inter-agency cooperation and ensure a more effective response to potential threats.
2. Enhanced Monitoring of Known Extremists:
	 The inadequate monitoring of known extremists, such as Fejzulai, was a critical failure in the Vienna attack, emphasizing the importance of continuous supervision and support for released offenders.
	 Comprehensive and evidence-based deradicalization programs, with a focus on addressing the root causes of extremism and providing ongoing monitoring, can help to minimize the risk of re-offending and promote successful reintegration.
3. More Rigorous Approaches to Deradicalization:
	 The failure to assess the true level of deradicalization in Fejzulai's case underscored the need for more rigorous and tailored approaches to rehabilitation and reintegration.
	 Individualized deradicalization plans, with a focus on addressing the unique needs and circumstances of each offender, can help to ensure that programs are effective and adaptable to the evolving threat landscape.
4. Improved Coordination Among Security Agencies:
	 The lack of coordination among security agencies in the response to the Vienna attack highlighted the need for regular joint exercises and training programs to enhance inter-agency cooperation and communication.
	 Comprehensive emergency response plans, with clear frameworks for action and adaptation, can help to ensure a swift and effective response to a wide range of threats and challenges.
5. Greater Focus on Online Radicalization:
	 The insufficient focus on online radicalization in the case of the Vienna attack emphasized the need for greater emphasis on understanding and countering the role of the internet and social media in extremist recruitment and indoctrination.
	 Partnerships with tech companies to remove extremist content, as well as educational programs on digital literacy and critical thinking, can help to reduce the risk of radicalization and promote alternative narratives that emphasize peace, tolerance, and understanding.
6. Stricter Probation Conditions:
	 The inadequate probation conditions for Fejzulai in the Vienna attack highlighted the need for stricter restrictions and supervision for individuals convicted of terrorism-related offenses.
	 Regular check-ins with law enforcement, restrictions on travel and communication, and mandatory participation in deradicalization programs can help to prevent re-offending and ensure the successful reintegration of released offenders.
7. Addressing the Root Causes of Radicalization:
	 The failure to address the root causes of radicalization in the case of the Vienna attack underscored the importance of tackling the social, economic, and political grievances that can fuel extremism and terrorism.
	 Investment in education, job training, and social services, as well as the promotion of diversity and inclusion, can help to foster a sense of belonging and identity among all members of society and build resilient communities that are better equipped to prevent radicalization and respond to terrorist incidents.

Conclusion
The intelligence failures that contributed to the Vienna attack have been extensively analyzed and discussed in the years following the tragedy. These failures have highlighted the need for improved intelligence sharing, enhanced monitoring of known extremists, more rigorous approaches to deradicalization, better coordination among security agencies, greater focus on online radicalization, stricter probation conditions, and addressing the root causes of radicalization. By learning from these failures and implementing the necessary reforms and improvements, Austria and the international community can work towards preventing similar incidents in the future and promoting a more peaceful, secure, and resilient society.
