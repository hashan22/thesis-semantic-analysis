Key Themes and Narratives

1. Immigration and Integration:
	 Right-wing news outlets framed the Vienna attack as a result of failed immigration and integration policies, emphasizing the attacker's immigrant background and suggesting that such incidents are a direct consequence of lax immigration controls and misguided multiculturalism.
2. Islam and Terrorism:
	 These outlets focused heavily on the attacker's Muslim faith, using sensationalist language to link Islam with terrorism and extremism. This framing perpetuated stereotypes and contributed to Islamophobia, reinforcing the narrative that Islam is inherently violent and incompatible with Western values.
3. Cultural and Civilizational Conflict:
	 Right-wing news outlets framed the attack as part of a broader clash of civilizations, suggesting that Western and Islamic cultures are fundamentally incompatible. This narrative promoted the idea that strong borders, cultural assimilation, and resistance to multiculturalism are necessary to protect national identity and security.
4. Government and Policy Failures:
	 These outlets criticized the government's handling of the attack, suggesting that lax security measures, ineffective counterterrorism policies, and misguided deradicalization programs were to blame. This framing undermined public trust in government institutions and policies, promoting the idea that stronger, more authoritarian measures are necessary to ensure national security.
5. Lone Wolf Narrative:
	 Right-wing news outlets emphasized the lone wolf nature of the attack, suggesting that such incidents are difficult to prevent and that the threat of terrorism is pervasive and ever-present. This narrative reinforced the idea that increased surveillance and security measures are necessary to protect against the omnipresent threat of terrorism.
6. Victimhood and National Identity:
	 These outlets portrayed the nation and its citizens as victims of terrorism, emphasizing the need for unity, resilience, and a strong national identity. This framing promoted a narrative of national victimhood and resilience, emphasizing the importance of national unity and cultural homogeneity in the face of external threats.
7. Conspiracy Theories and Misinformation:
	 In some cases, right-wing news outlets promoted conspiracy theories and misinformation about the attack, suggesting that it was part of a larger plot or that the official narrative was being suppressed or manipulated. This framing contributed to a climate of distrust and skepticism, undermining public confidence in official accounts and mainstream media sources.

Conclusion
Right-wing news outlets framed the Vienna attack in a manner that emphasized immigration and integration failures, the link between Islam and terrorism, cultural and civilizational conflict, government and policy failures, the lone wolf narrative, national victimhood and identity, and conspiracy theories and misinformation. This framing reinforced specific political and ideological narratives, contributing to a climate of fear, distrust, and polarization. Understanding the framing of right-wing news outlets is essential for gaining a comprehensive and nuanced understanding of the media's role in shaping public perceptions and narratives about complex and sensitive issues like terrorism and extremism. By considering multiple perspectives and sources, readers can develop a more informed, critical, and constructive public discourse, fostering a more inclusive, cohesive, and resilient society.
