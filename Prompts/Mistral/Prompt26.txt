Key Shifts in Media Coverage
1.	From Breaking News to In-Depth Analysis:
	 In the immediate aftermath of the attack, media coverage focused on providing up-to-date information and ensuring public safety.
	 As the situation stabilized, coverage shifted towards in-depth analysis of the attack, its causes, and its implications for Austrian society and European security.
2. From Victim Stories to Policy Debates:
	 In the days following the attack, media outlets highlighted the stories of the victims and their families, humanizing the tragedy and fostering empathy.
	 As the government announced new security measures and policy changes, coverage shifted towards debates about the effectiveness and implications of these proposals.
3. From Unity to Division:
	 In the immediate aftermath of the attack, media coverage emphasized the sense of unity and solidarity among Viennese residents and the international community.
	 As political debates and policy discussions intensified, coverage began to reflect the growing divisions and controversies surrounding issues such as immigration, integration, and the role of Islam in Austrian society.
4. From Sensationalism to Reflection:
	 In the early days of the coverage, some media outlets focused on sensational aspects of the story, such as the attacker's background and the graphic details of the attack.
	 As time passed, coverage became more reflective and nuanced, with a focus on the long-term impact of the attack, the lessons learned, and the stories of healing and resilience.

The media coverage of the Vienna attack evolved significantly over time, reflecting the changing priorities, narratives, and public sentiment in the aftermath of the tragedy.
