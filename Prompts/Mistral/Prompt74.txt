Using language responsibly and thoughtfully is crucial to avoid sensationalizing terrorism and to ensure that reporting and discussions about such events are accurate, respectful, and constructive. Here are some guidelines and principles for using language in a way that avoids sensationalism:
Guideline	Details	Implications
Avoid Emotional and Loaded Language	- Use neutral, factual, and precise language to describe the event, the individuals involved, and the broader context.
- Avoid emotional, dramatic, or loaded terms that can heighten fear, anxiety, or stereotypes, such as "monster," "evil," or "barbaric."	Ensures that the language used is informative, balanced, and responsible, fostering a more thoughtful, empathetic, and constructive understanding of the event and its impact.
Focus on Facts and Context	- Prioritize accurate, reliable, and verified information, providing the necessary context and background to help the audience understand the event and its broader implications.
- Avoid speculation, rumors, or unverified claims that can cause confusion, misinformation, or harm.	Promotes a more informed, critical, and nuanced understanding of the event, fostering a sense of trust, confidence, and responsibility among the audience.
Humanize the Victims and Affected Communities	- Use language that emphasizes the humanity, dignity, and experiences of the victims, their families, and affected communities.
- Avoid reducing the victims to statistics, symbols, or stereotypes, and instead, highlight their stories, values, and legacies.	Fosters a sense of empathy, connection, and solidarity among the audience, ensuring that the victims are remembered and honored in a respectful and meaningful way.
Avoid Glorifying or Sensationalizing the Attacker	- Refrain from using language that can inadvertently glorify, sensationalize, or humanize the attacker, such as providing excessive details about their background, motivations, or actions.
- Avoid using the attacker's name, image, or manifesto repeatedly, as this can contribute to their notoriety and potential inspiration for others.	Prevents the potential for copycat attacks, the spread of extremist ideologies, and the retraumatization of victims, survivors, and affected communities.
Be Culturally and Religiously Sensitive	- Use language that is culturally and religiously sensitive, recognizing the diverse backgrounds, experiences, and needs of the individuals and communities involved.
- Avoid stereotypes, generalizations, or stigmatizing terms that can perpetuate divisions, fears, or discrimination.	Promotes a more inclusive, tolerant, and empathetic understanding of the event and its broader context, fostering a sense of respect, understanding, and unity among the audience.
Promote Resilience, Unity, and Hope	- Use language that emphasizes the themes of resilience, unity, and hope, highlighting the strength, determination, and solidarity of the victims, survivors, and affected communities.
- Avoid language that can foster despair, helplessness, or polarization, and instead, focus on the constructive and proactive responses to the event.	Encourages a sense of empowerment, inspiration, and commitment among the audience, fostering a more resilient, cohesive, and just society.
Encourage Critical Thinking and Media Literacy	- Use language that promotes critical thinking, media literacy, and digital citizenship, helping the audience to navigate the complex and sensitive issues related to the event.
- Avoid language that can oversimplify, misinform, or manipulate, and instead, provide the necessary information, context, and resources for the audience to form their own informed and thoughtful opinions.	Fosters a more engaged, discerning, and responsible audience, equipped with the knowledge, skills, and attitudes necessary to address the challenges and opportunities of a diverse and interconnected world.
