Addressing the Vienna Attack with Students: A Teacher's Perspective
By [Your Name], Public School Teacher, Vienna
Vienna, November 2020 — As a public school teacher in Vienna, the terrorist attack that occurred on November 2, 2020, presented a significant challenge in addressing the event with my students. It was crucial to create a safe and supportive environment where students could express their feelings, ask questions, and process the incident in a healthy and constructive manner. Here is how I approached this sensitive topic with my students.
Creating a Safe Space
On the morning after the attack, I began the day by acknowledging the event and expressing my condolences to those affected. I emphasized that our classroom was a safe space where everyone's feelings and thoughts were valid and respected. I encouraged students to share their emotions, whether they were feeling scared, confused, angry, or sad.
Age-Appropriate Discussions
I tailored my approach based on the age and maturity level of my students. For younger students, I focused on providing reassurance and emphasizing safety. I explained that the police and other adults were working hard to keep us safe and that it was okay to feel upset or worried.
For older students, I facilitated a more in-depth discussion about the event, its impact on our community, and the importance of unity and resilience. I encouraged them to ask questions and express their thoughts, while also providing accurate and age-appropriate information to address any misconceptions or fears.
Addressing Fears and Concerns
Many students expressed fears about their safety and the possibility of future attacks. I acknowledged their concerns and validated their feelings, emphasizing that it was normal to feel anxious after such an event. I also reassured them that our school and community were taking steps to ensure their safety and that it was important to focus on the positive aspects of our lives and the support we have from one another.
Promoting Unity and Empathy
I used this opportunity to promote unity, empathy, and understanding among my students. We discussed the importance of standing together as a community, supporting one another, and rejecting hatred and division. I encouraged students to reach out to their classmates, friends, and family members, offering kindness and support to those who may be struggling.
We also talked about the heroes of the event—the first responders, the strangers who helped others, and the community members who came together to support one another. I highlighted the acts of bravery and compassion that emerged from the tragedy, emphasizing that these actions represented the true spirit of Vienna.
Encouraging Expression and Healing
I provided various outlets for students to express their feelings and process the event. We engaged in art activities, creative writing, and group discussions, allowing students to share their thoughts and emotions in a supportive environment. I also encouraged students to talk to their families, friends, or school counselors if they needed additional support.
Providing Resources and Support
I made sure to provide students with information about the resources and support available to them, both within the school and in the community. I reminded them that our school counselors, social workers, and other mental health professionals were there to help them cope with their feelings and navigate this difficult time.
I also shared information about community events and initiatives aimed at promoting healing, unity, and resilience. I encouraged students to participate in these events, emphasizing the importance of coming together as a community to support one another and honor the memory of those affected by the attack.
Fostering Resilience and Hope
Throughout our discussions, I emphasized the importance of resilience and hope. I reminded students that, despite the tragedy, our community was strong and united. I encouraged them to focus on the positive aspects of their lives, to support one another, and to work together to build a brighter future.
I also highlighted the role that young people can play in promoting peace, understanding, and compassion. I encouraged students to be ambassadors of kindness and empathy, to stand up against hatred and division, and to work towards creating a more inclusive and supportive community.
Conclusion
Addressing the Vienna attack with my students was a challenging but essential task. By creating a safe and supportive environment, providing age-appropriate information, and promoting unity, empathy, and resilience, I aimed to help my students process the event in a healthy and constructive manner.
As a teacher, my role is not only to educate but also to support and guide my students through difficult times. The Vienna attack was a stark reminder of the importance of fostering a sense of community, empathy, and resilience in our classrooms. By standing together and supporting one another, we can help our students navigate the challenges they face and build a brighter future.
